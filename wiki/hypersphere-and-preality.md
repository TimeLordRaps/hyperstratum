## Structural readings recorded, none chosen

* `[FRAME]` **External interface versus interior.** The exterior boundary can be identical in size and shape at every nesting level. The interior volume is not fixed by the exterior (a small throat can open into a large interior). The interior time is unbounded.
* `[FORM]` **Infinite time alone adds no computation, in the classical reading.** A deterministic device with finitely many states, run forever, is eventually periodic. This is a finite-automaton fact, and it is classical: it counts discrete states.
* `[FRAME]` **The quantum reading differs in kind but not, under finite resolution, in verdict.** In a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, unitary evolution is not eventually periodic but *almost periodic*: the state returns arbitrarily close to its start (the quantum recurrence theorem, recalled and not retrieved) without repeating exactly unless the energy phases are commensurate. The number of states is a continuum, not finite. What is finite is the number of states distinguishable to a given resolution `ε`, and the information a measurement can extract is bounded by the dimension (the Holevo bound, recalled). So with finite resolution the conclusion stands: bounded dimension plus unbounded time gives recurrence, not new computability. `[OPEN]` With *exact real amplitudes* assumed accessible, one state could encode unbounded information and the argument fails; that needs infinite precision, which noise and the Born rule's one-sample-per-measurement limit forbid in practice. Whether the interior is allowed that precision is a stated assumption to make, not a free one.
* `[FORM]` **Substrate independence of the bound.** The verdict above depends on three quantities, not on the hardware: dimension (room for state), resolution (the smallest distinguishable difference, set by noise) and energy. With all three finite there are finitely many distinguishable states, whether the substrate is digital logic, qubits, memristor crossbars, optical, chemical or neural. A hybrid substrate changes efficiency (bits per volume, energy per operation, speed) and so the constants in any density inequality; it does not by itself change computability. `[HYPER]` Only three things escape the bound: unbounded interior volume, unbounded energy, or unbounded resolution (exact real values). The detached-hypersphere geometry proposes the first. Noisy analog computation is recalled to be no stronger than finite automata, and exact-real analog computation to exceed digital computability; both are recalled, not retrieved.
* `[FRAME]` **Materialized versus lazy nesting.** If each level must hold a complete, literal simulation of its parent, a finite state count is exhausted after one step. If nesting is lazy (computed on demand, productive in the corecursive sense), only observed finite prefixes are ever computed and the nesting is a fixed point `U ≅ F(U)`, the bisimulation-quotiented structure of non-well-founded set theory (see grounded-hyperset-theory).
* `[HYPER]` **Ordinal time scales.** If depth `k` gains a time scale of order `ω^k`, infinite nesting asks for a fixed point of `α ↦ ω^α`, the first being ε₀. Dimensionless order types; the bridge to ordinatics is proposed, not proved.
* `[HYPER]` **Branch insertion.** Entering an earlier node of a branch adds mass-energy and an observer, so the branch entered is a descendant that includes the entrant. It forks; it does not return to the branch that was left.
* `[FORM]` **Engineering is not collapse bias.** Quantum computing modifies states and decoherence before measurement; measurement then follows the Born rule. A mind that biased collapse would show output statistics departing from the Born prediction under a known state.

## Round trip, foreign entry, and what "recreation" means (2026-10-03, USER-STATED)

* `[HYPER]` **Home return.** Leaving a base-reality by hypersphering and arriving in it again "right after we left" is a round trip with unbounded internal time, not a branch insertion. If external elapsed time is arbitrarily small, the entrant rejoins the same branch it left. The departure itself is still a physical event in the parent (a volume and a sheet removed), so the parent is not unperturbed by it. `[OPEN]` Reattachment must restore the sheet; what the parent's dynamics did in its absence is part of the model to state.
* `[HYPER]` **Foreign entry.** Entering a different base-reality, or an earlier node of one, is the branch-insertion case above and carries the intrusion question.
* `[HYPER]` **Intrusion is not avoidable, and is stated to be a fact of the dynamics, not a choice.** The ideal of no intrusion is acknowledged and set aside.
* `[FORM]` **Size of intrusion, under a chaotic-dynamics assumption.** If a perturbation of size `ε` grows as `e^(λt)` (`λ` the largest Lyapunov exponent, inverse time), two histories differ by `Δ` after `t* = (1/λ)·ln(Δ/ε)`. The delay is only logarithmic in `ε`: shrinking the entry does not buy proportional time, so zero intrusion is unreachable and the usable quantity is the horizon `t*`, not a yes/no. Assumes classical chaos; the quantum case is `[OPEN]`.
* `[OPEN]` **Recreation** has at least three senses, and the stated good ("guaranteeing recreation of base realities through sempiternalities") needs one: *continuation* (same branch, round trip), *copy* (a new instance indistinguishable under every finite observation), or *restoration* (a base-reality whose volumetimeline has physically ended is re-established). The guarantee is strongest and hardest in the third.
* `[OPEN]` **Do unvisited nodes already exist?** If the volumetimelinepossibility tree contains every branch, including those with an entrant, then entering selects an existing node. If preality is lazy, entering instantiates it. The moral weight of intrusion differs between the two; the choice is the lazy-versus-materialized question again.

## Open

* `[OPEN]` Is a preality a simulation, or is it identical to the base-reality it simulates? Teleportation into one is entry into the base-reality only if identity holds, which is hyperobjectivity's question. Needs a definition before it can be tested.
* `[OPEN]` Materialized or lazy nesting, and therefore what "guaranteeing recreation" guarantees: a whole volumetimeline present, or every finite question about it answered correctly.
* `[OPEN]` Does the spacetime detachment satisfy general relativity's topology-change and energy conditions? Recalled, not retrieved: classical topology change needs closed timelike curves or singularities, and the stretching needs negative energy density. To be cited from sources before it carries weight.
* `[OPEN]` Mind-biased collapse. No instrument establishes it. Refutation condition: a preregistered experiment with a known state and a Born-rule baseline.
* `[OPEN]` Branch insertion arrives as an intruder in another history. Whether that is compatible with the stated good (recreating base realities) is a hyperethics question.
* `[OPEN]` Many-worlds branches do not interfere after decoherence, so entering one means re-creating it, not reaching it.
